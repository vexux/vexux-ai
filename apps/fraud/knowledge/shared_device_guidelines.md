# Shared-Device Investigation Guidelines

This document is project reference guidance for the Vexux-AI fraud
demonstration. A shared-device relationship is an investigative lead, not an
official risk rule or a conclusion about any particular customer.

## Investigative significance

A graph relationship indicating that customers used the same device can help
identify a connection worth reviewing. The relationship alone does not show
who controlled the device, whether the activity was authorized, or whether
either customer acted improperly.

## Legitimate and suspicious explanations

Legitimate explanations may include a shared household device, a public or
work device, accessibility support, or an account used with consent.
Potentially concerning explanations depend on context, such as closely timed
activity that is inconsistent with other records or multiple independent
signals associated with the same device. Neither explanation should be
assumed without corroboration.

## Corroboration and assessment

Verify the underlying relationship and its source. Consider relevant
transaction timing, alert records, account ownership, and other independently
observed connections. Distinguish direct observations from hypotheses, and
note missing or conflicting evidence.

A shared-device relationship by itself must not determine a final fraud
assessment or numeric score. The application's deterministic workflow and
authoritative operational sources remain responsible for customer-specific
evidence and assessment. Use this guidance to frame questions for review, not
to replace those sources.
